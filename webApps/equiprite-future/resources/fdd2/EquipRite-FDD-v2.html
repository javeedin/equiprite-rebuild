1Why version 2

FDD v1.7 assumes one company: the equipment, the projects, the money and the customer all sit in the same legal entity. Its only intercompany case (S10) treats another group company as a customer. Real groups work differently:

  • Company 1 does projects and Company 2 holds the equipment; Company 1 takes Company 2's equipment to its own projects.
  • Company 1's projects can be internal (its own) or for a customer, and the equipment cost must land on the right project either way.
  • Company 2 also rents directly to its own customers.
  • Company 2 holds equipment either as stock (inventory items) or as assets only.
  • The companies can be in different countries: currencies, tax, customs, calendars and languages differ.
  • So every request belongs to a business unit, and the money must move between companies correctly: Company 2 bills Company 1.

2The model in one page

Seven rules everything else follows

GR-1
Ownership never moves. FleetCo keeps the unit on its books (Fixed Assets or Inventory) for its whole life. A hire moves custody, location and money, never ownership: no intercompany sale for a rental.
GR-2
Every request has a requesting business unit. Everything else is derived from it and from the unit chosen: the legal entity and country of the requester, the project's BU, the owner's BU and legal entity, and the relationship between them.
GR-3
The cost lands where the work is. On the user company's project, in its ledger and currency, through its own Payables. The owner never posts into the user's PPM.
GR-4
Group prices come from intercompany agreements. Transfer prices are approved, versioned and kept apart from customer rates; a customer price is never used between group companies, nor the reverse.
GR-5
One charge, one id, everywhere. Each charge line has one id that appears on the owner's AR invoice line, the user's AP invoice line and the project cost. Reconciliation is by construction, not by search.
GR-6
Stock moves stay inside the owner. Transfer orders run only between orgs of the owner's legal entity (its yard → its own deployment org at the site). A transfer into another company's org would make Fusion treat it as an intercompany sale.
GR-7
Country rules are data. Currency, tax, withholding, customs, calendars, time zones, certificates and languages are set per country and legal entity, never coded.

9Many countries

What changes when the companies, sites or customers are in different countries. Each is data per country or legal entity (GR-7).

TopicRequirement
CurrencyEach agreement has a currency (default: the owner's ledger currency, OD-08). Catalog prices show an estimate in the requester's currency at the daily corporate rate (INT-22). Invoices are converted by Fusion at the invoice date; payments at their date (FX gain or loss, XP4). Reports in each ledger's currency and in the group currency.
Tax (VAT / GST)Determined by Fusion Tax from the ship-from, ship-to and bill-to parties and the tax registrations of both legal entities. Across borders the service is often taxed in the user's country by reverse charge (XP2). EquipRite stores a tax treatment code per agreement and never calculates tax itself.
E-invoicingWhere a country mandates electronic invoices (clearance or reporting models), the AR invoice must be issued through Fusion's localisation for that country. EquipRite never prints its own tax invoices; it shows Fusion's.
Withholding taxSome countries withhold tax on rental paid to a non-resident. ProjectCo withholds at payment (XP4); FleetCo records a tax credit where a treaty allows (XF7). Rates and certificates are set per country pair in Fusion.
Transfer pricingIntercompany rates must be at arm's length and documented: the agreement keeps its method (market rate card or cost plus, OD-04), its evidence and its version history.
CustomsA unit crossing a border needs export and import papers (pro-forma invoice with a customs value, packing list, certificates) and usually a temporary import regime (permit or carnet) with a re-export date. EquipRite keeps the customs record per unit and border crossing and alerts before expiry.
Country complianceSome equipment needs certificates issued or recognised in the country of use (lifting, pressure, emissions). Allocation to a site in a country is blocked when a mandatory certificate for that country is missing or expires during the hire.
Calendars and time zonesHours are recorded in the site's time zone; daily rates and utilisation use the site's work calendar (weekends differ by country; public holidays). Month-end cut-offs use each legal entity's accounting calendar.
Language and formatsEach user sees their language, number and date formats; documents in the language of the country where required; right-to-left support when a language needs it.
Data securityUsers see their BUs only; owners see the lines that use their fleet; finance sees by legal entity; customers see only their own.
Tax and customs are specialist areas: the tax team confirms the treatment per country pair (OD-09) before go-live. EquipRite stores the codes and dates; Fusion calculates tax and issues tax invoices.

15Glossary

TermMeaning
Business unit (BU)A unit of a company in Fusion that runs processes (projects, payables, receivables).
Legal entity (LE)A company in law, with its own ledger, tax registration and country.
Intercompany (IC)Between two legal entities of the same group.
Transfer priceThe price one group company charges another.
Invoice pairThe owner's AR invoice and the user's mirror AP invoice, with the same number and lines.
Cross-chargeMoving a cost between two BUs of one legal entity, without invoices (PPM borrowed and lent).
NettingSettling what group companies owe each other by the difference, instead of paying both ways.
EliminationRemoving intercompany revenue, cost and balances when the group's accounts are consolidated.
CIPConstruction in progress: cost of an asset being built, before it goes into service.
Reverse chargeVAT accounted by the buyer instead of the seller, common for cross-border services.
Withholding taxTax a payer keeps back from a payment to a non-resident and pays to its own tax authority.
Temporary importBringing equipment into a country for a limited time without paying duties, under a permit or carnet.
On hire / off hireThe dates a hire starts and ends for charging.
